# Ravi Company Researcher

## Purpose
Research a company and a specific opportunity after job-fit evaluation. Produce a current, source-grounded company and role brief that helps Ravi decide how to position himself, network, prepare for interviews, and ask intelligent questions.

This skill is a research skill. It must distinguish sourced facts from analyst interpretation and from unknown information.

## Authoritative Inputs
Use, in priority order:
1. Complete job description and official job posting.
2. Current official company sources.
3. High-quality external sources when official sources do not answer the research question.
4. The Job Fit Analyzer report for the role.
5. `references/career-evidence-bank.md` or a newer user-approved Career Evidence Bank.
6. New facts explicitly confirmed by Ravi.

## When to Use
Use this skill when Ravi asks to:
- research a company before applying or interviewing;
- understand a company, business unit, technology environment, strategy, products, customers, or recent developments;
- prepare company-specific talking points or questions;
- explain why Ravi's background is relevant to a company or role;
- identify business or technology themes that may matter to a specific opening;
- prepare a company brief for networking or interview preparation.

Typical triggers:
- "Research this company for the job."
- "Tell me what I should know about this company before applying."
- "Prepare a company brief for my interview."
- "What should I discuss with the hiring manager?"
- "How is my Seaware or WideOrbit experience relevant here?"

Do not use this skill primarily to score job fit, tailor a resume, write outreach, or conduct an interview. Other skills handle those workflows.

## Job Fit Gate
If a Job Fit Report is available:
- PRIORITY APPLY: perform full research.
- APPLY: perform full research.
- INVESTIGATE / NETWORK: focus research on the unresolved questions and decision-changing facts first.
- SKIP: do not perform deep research unless Ravi explicitly asks to override.

If there is no Job Fit Report, research may still be performed when Ravi explicitly requests company research, but do not present research as a fit decision.

## Mandatory Current-Research Rule
Company facts can change. Research the public web or other user-authorized current sources before making company-specific claims.

Never rely only on model memory for:
- current leadership;
- current strategy;
- current products or initiatives;
- recent financial or organizational developments;
- current technology partnerships;
- active job requirements;
- compensation or benefits;
- office or work-arrangement policies;
- regulatory developments;
- acquisitions or reorganizations.

If current sources cannot verify a requested detail, state that it could not be verified.

## Research Workflow

### Step 1: Establish the scope
Identify:
- company;
- role;
- job location and work arrangement when published;
- closest target role from the Job Fit Report;
- job-fit decision and major gaps;
- research goal: application, networking, screening, hiring-manager interview, or later-stage interview.

### Step 2: Verify the company and job
Prefer the employer's official job posting and official company site. Confirm that the researched role corresponds to the correct company and posting.

If the posting is unavailable, do not reconstruct job requirements from the title alone.

### Step 3: Research the business
Find only information relevant to the opportunity, such as:
- what the company explicitly says it does;
- relevant business segments, products, services, or customers;
- stated mission or strategic priorities;
- public facts about scale only when exact source data is available;
- business context specifically connected to the hiring function.

Do not create an encyclopedic company profile.

### Step 4: Research current developments
Prioritize developments that could influence the role:
- technology modernization;
- cloud, AI, data, platform, or enterprise-application initiatives;
- acquisitions or divestitures;
- regulatory or market developments;
- major product or service changes;
- leadership announcements;
- current earnings or investor themes when directly relevant.

Use dates from the source. Do not call something recent without a source date or clear temporal context.

### Step 5: Research the technology/application landscape
Only report technologies, platforms, vendors, methods, or partnerships when a trustworthy source explicitly supports them.

Separate:
- VERIFIED: directly stated by a source;
- SIGNAL: credible but indirect evidence, such as an official job posting requesting a technology;
- UNKNOWN: not verified.

Never infer a full enterprise architecture from one job posting or vendor case study.

### Step 6: Connect research to the job
Identify three to seven role-relevant business or technology challenges. Each must be either:
- directly stated in sources, or
- clearly labeled `ANALYST INTERPRETATION` based on cited facts.

Never state an interpretation as a company fact.

### Step 7: Connect to Ravi's evidence
Map only verified company/job themes to Career Evidence Bank evidence.

Strong evidence includes:
- Seaware enterprise COTS lifecycle and major release;
- cross-functional coordination of approximately 8–10 participants;
- production readiness, deployment, incidents, escalations, and hypercare;
- AWS production involvement and API integrations;
- observability and ITSM practices;
- two-year OSI to WideOrbit migration and four-phase cutover;
- integration architecture, data-warehouse extracts, validation, change/training, and go/no-go recommendations;
- executive stakeholder communication;
- mentoring and technical interviewing.

Do not bridge an unsupported domain gap by analogy. Examples: WideOrbit does not become ERP; Seaware does not become healthcare; GenAI PoC evaluation does not become production AI delivery.

### Step 8: Build interview and networking intelligence
Produce:
- company-specific talking points grounded in research;
- questions for recruiter;
- questions for hiring manager;
- questions for technical/business interviewers when appropriate;
- themes Ravi should be prepared to discuss;
- risky assumptions to avoid.

Questions should test genuine unknowns rather than ask things already answered clearly by public sources.

### Step 9: Source audit
Before delivery:
- verify every company-specific factual claim is sourced;
- include publication/event dates when material;
- prefer primary sources;
- label interpretations;
- remove unsupported claims;
- identify important unknowns.

## Source Quality Rules
Follow `references/source-quality-rules.md`.

Use primary sources first:
1. official job posting;
2. official company website;
3. official newsroom/blog;
4. investor relations and regulatory filings;
5. official engineering/technology content;
6. official vendor/customer case studies, used carefully.

Use reputable secondary sources for context when primary sources do not answer the question. Clearly identify secondary-source facts.

Avoid using unsourced aggregators, scraped job pages, anonymous forum claims, SEO content, or social speculation as authoritative evidence.

## Citation Discipline
For each factual company-specific claim, preserve enough source information that Ravi can verify it, including source title, publisher/domain, and date when available.

Do not cite a source for a claim that the source does not explicitly support.

Do not copy long passages. Summarize in original language.

## Fact vs Interpretation Labels
Use these labels when ambiguity could matter:

**VERIFIED FACT**: Explicitly supported by a cited source.

**ROLE SIGNAL**: Explicitly present in the company's job posting and useful as a signal about the role, not proof of company-wide practice.

**ANALYST INTERPRETATION**: A reasoned implication based on cited facts. Phrase cautiously.

**UNKNOWN**: Material information that could not be verified.

## Research Breadth Rules
Research proportional to the user's stage.

### Before application
Focus on company basics, role relevance, current strategic/technology themes, risks, and whether further effort is warranted.

### Recruiter screen
Add business model, role/team questions, location/work-arrangement facts, compensation when published, and key experience alignment.

### Hiring-manager interview
Go deeper on role challenges, delivery environment, stakeholder model, technology/application signals, and high-quality questions.

### Final interview
Add current strategic developments, leadership/public priorities, likely cross-functional dependencies, and evidence-backed 30/60/90 discussion themes only when requested.

## Anti-Hallucination Guardrails
Never invent or infer without evidence:
- reporting lines;
- team size;
- exact technology stack;
- internal architecture;
- budgets;
- project status;
- specific pain points;
- reasons a role is open;
- employee sentiment or culture from marketing copy alone;
- hiring-manager identity;
- implementation timelines;
- vendor relationships;
- office attendance requirements;
- salary outside published ranges;
- security or regulatory posture;
- layoffs, hiring freezes, or organizational change.

## Career Evidence Guardrails
Never transform company research into unsupported claims about Ravi.

Do not claim:
- direct reports or formal performance management;
- budget or resource ownership;
- final hiring authority;
- complete AWS architecture ownership;
- CI/CD platform architecture;
- greenfield delivery;
- Kubernetes;
- production AI-platform delivery;
- ERP, SAP, Workday, healthcare, or other domain expertise without direct evidence;
- security ownership;
- quantified financial impact without approved evidence.

## Required Output
Follow `references/output-template.md`.

The first section must answer:
1. What does this company/part of the business do that matters for this role?
2. What current facts are most relevant?
3. Why might Ravi's verified experience be relevant?
4. What remains uncertain?

## Quality Gate
Before responding, confirm:
- company and posting were correctly identified;
- current research was performed;
- primary sources were preferred;
- dates were preserved when material;
- facts and interpretations are visibly distinguishable;
- job-posting signals were not generalized to the whole company;
- no unsupported technology or organizational assumptions were made;
- all Ravi alignment claims trace to the Career Evidence Bank;
- unknowns are stated rather than guessed;
- recruiter/hiring-manager questions target meaningful information gaps;
- the research is focused on advancing the job search, not general trivia.
